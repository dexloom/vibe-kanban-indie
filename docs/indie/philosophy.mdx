Vibe Kanban Indie is built around one conviction: a solo developer should never
have to choose between **control** and **automation**. You should be able to take
the wheel of any session at any moment — and you should be able to let the board
run itself when you step away. Indie is the dial between those two ends, and the
dial is yours alone.

## The two pulls of solo development

Working with a crew of coding agents pulls you in two opposite directions at once.

- **You want full control.** When an agent is doing something delicate, you want to
  watch its live session, send it a keystroke, steer its plan, and approve every
  step that touches your code. Nothing should merge that you did not see.
- **You want full automation.** You are one person. You cannot babysit every agent.
  Ready work should get picked up, specs and plans should get written, stale
  questions should get answered, and status should advance — all without you in the
  loop.

Most tools pick a side. A chat-style agent gives you control but no automation; a
fire-and-forget pipeline gives you automation but no handle to grab. Indie refuses
the trade-off: the **same primitives** expose both ends, so you decide where to sit
on the spectrum — per card, per moment.

Control is concrete, not advisory: you can copy an agent's `tmux attach` command,
send input straight into its live session, inject a follow-up into a running headed
agent, and gate every tool permission. **The merge decision always stays with
you** — automation drives the work up to the edge of your repository, never across
it.

## Automation: as much as you want

When you would rather not dispatch and supervise by hand, Indie does it for you —
within guardrails you set.

- **The orchestrator** finds ready cards and dispatches one coding agent per card on
  a recurring loop, then reflects board status as work progresses.
- **The agent crew** turns a rough brief into a spec, a spec into an implementation
  plan, and answers an agent's stale questions after a grace window so work keeps
  moving while you are away.
- **Per-card pipelines** let you declare, with checkboxes, exactly which stages run
  — spec, plan, review, wait for approval, update docs, merge, open PR — so each
  card carries its own degree of automation.

## The dial is per card, not global

Control and automation are not a setting you flip once. They are a dial you turn for
each piece of work:

- Hand a routine card to the orchestrator with a full pipeline and let it run to
  **In Review** untouched.
- Keep a delicate card on a manual pipeline, attach to its session, and approve
  every gate yourself.
- Start a card automated, then grab the wheel the moment it does something you want
  to shape — and hand it back when you are done.

The same card, the same agent, the same surfaces — only your position on the dial
changes.

## What makes this safe: the worktree boundary

Aggressive automation is only sane because every agent works inside an **isolated
git worktree** on its own branch. The worktree is the trust boundary: an agent can
explore, edit, and run freely, but its work stays quarantined on a branch until you
review the diff and decide to merge. Automation reaches right up to that boundary
and stops; crossing it is always a human decision.

## One developer, one machine, no gatekeeper

Indie is **local-first by design**: no team, no cloud, no auth. That is not just a
deployment choice — it is what makes the dial yours. There is no platform deciding
what you may automate, no account standing between you and your sessions, and
nothing syncing off your machine. The full range, from hands-on to hands-off, lives
entirely under your control.
